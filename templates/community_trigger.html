What is the Community Trigger?

The Community Trigger — officially called an ASB Case Review — is a legal right created by the Anti-Social Behaviour, Crime and Policing Act 2014. It gives victims of persistent anti-social behaviour the right to demand a formal review of how their case has been handled by the relevant agencies.

If you have made a qualifying number of reports to a council, housing provider, or police — and they have failed to take adequate action — you can apply to your local council to trigger a review. The council must then bring together all the relevant agencies to look at what has happened and agree a plan.

Despite being a legal right, research suggests that fewer than 1 in 17 people affected by persistent ASB are aware it exists. Many cases that could qualify are never reviewed, simply because victims don't know they can ask.

The exact threshold (number of reports, time period) can vary slightly by council, but three qualifying reports within a 12-month period is the standard national threshold set in the statutory guidance.
